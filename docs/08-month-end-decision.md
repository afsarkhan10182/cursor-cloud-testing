# Month-end analysis and months 2–3 decision

## Success is not “we sold X”

At the end of 30 days we should be able to answer:

1. Which products attract interest?
2. Which customer segments respond?
3. Which channels work?
4. Which outreach messages work?
5. Which products generate demos?
6. What customers are willing to pay?
7. SaaS vs one-time preference?
8. Common objections?
9. Missing features blocking sales?
10. Which product deserves more investment?

## Conversion rates (required)

- **Response rate** = Responses ÷ Contacts
- **Demo rate** = Demos ÷ Responses
- **Proposal rate** = Proposals ÷ Demos
- **Close rate** = Sales ÷ Demos
- **Average revenue per customer**
- **CAC** (when we have spend: retainer + incentives + tools + any ads)

Raw volume can lie. A product with 20 leads and 3 serious proposals beats a product with 200 ignored WhatsApps.

## Decision rules (months 2–3)

### Strong winner

One product produces substantially more **qualified** opportunities (not just replies).

Then move **60–70%** of sales and content effort to that product. Keep small experiments elsewhere.

### Two products work

Concentrate on those two. Pause the rest of outbound. Keep a “if they ask” inbound path.

### Nothing works

Do **not** hire more people.

Diagnose the broken stage, in order:

1. Lead targeting (wrong businesses)
2. Messaging (right businesses, dead openers)
3. Product (demo does not match the pain)
4. Demo skill
5. Pricing
6. Trust (website, brand, founder availability)
7. Closing / follow-up

Fix the first broken stage. Then run another 30 days.

## Concentration test

A product deserves more investment if **at least two** of these are true:

- Demo booked rate is clearly higher than the others
- Prospects accept recurring pricing without a fight
- Customization asks are small
- Sales cycle is shorter
- Support load looks survivable for founders with jobs

A product with lots of curiosity and no willingness to pay is a hobby, not a winner.
